NAME THE HANDOFF, NOT JUST THE CHORE
“I'll do the laundry”
can mean different things.
One possible interpretation
One person starts the machine. Someone else checks the care labels, notices a missing item, moves the load and puts it away.
A clearer proposal
“Could we talk through one load from noticing it to putting it away, then decide which parts each of us is willing to own?”
There is no required split. One person might own the whole task, different stages might have different owners, or you might choose to revisit the plan later. This page does not compare effort or decide what is fair.
Make “together” specific.
If a stage is shared, use its detail field to describe the handoff. For example: “Person A checks the cupboard; Person B adds the agreed items to the shopping list.” Leaving it vague can hide the decision you still need to make.

Keep the conversation voluntary.
Does assigning a name mean they agreed?
No. Every assignment is labelled as proposed. A copied worksheet cannot confirm what another person has accepted.
What if we do not want the same split?
You can leave a stage “To discuss,” change the draft or stop. The worksheet does not choose a winner or settle a disagreement.
Can this replace a full household plan?
No. It describes one task. It does not measure time, caregiving, paid work, capacity or every other responsibility in a household.
Are our details saved or sent?
No. The tool does not submit or store entries. Copying places the proposal on your clipboard; you decide whether to share it.

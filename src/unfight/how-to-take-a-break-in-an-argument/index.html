Dear person who goes quiet mid-argument and then gets told that is the problem,

You asked how to take a break during an argument without it landing as stonewalling, because every time you step away it seems to make things worse. The short answer is that a break and a wall look identical from the other side of the room unless you do two things: say when you will be back, and come back. Everything below is the long answer, including a tool for the first of those and the honest limits of the app we make, UnFight.

    Questions you asked next

    How long should a break during an argument be?

    The Gottman Institute recommends allowing at least 20 minutes for self-soothing after someone feels flooded, and notes that some people need longer. Treat that as a starting point rather than a personal medical threshold. UnFight's break timer offers 5, 20 or 30 minutes, custom, or none.

    What is the difference between a break and stonewalling?

    A stated need for space plus a realistic return plan can make the intention clearer. No single sentence guarantees how a break will feel to the other person.

    What should I do during the break?

    The cited source recommends shifting attention away from the argument instead of rehearsing responses. Options can include a walk, another room or a grounding activity. UnFight's Calm Down has a quick pause, a guided pause and plain grounding for people who dislike breathing exercises; sound is off by default.

    What if my partner thinks a break is me walking out?

    Say when you plan to return, choose a time you can realistically keep and revisit the arrangement when both people are calmer. A script cannot guarantee how the break will be received, and an app cannot repair a harmful pattern on its own.

Thursday, box seventeen

A composite scenario from couples we have talked with, not a customer story.

Three weeks in. Priya has lived alone for six years and keeps the kitchen counter empty because an empty counter is how she knows the day is over. Dan has lived with housemates for six years and keeps the kitchen counter as a shelf because that is what counters are for. Tonight the counter has his bike pump on it, and the sentence that comes out of her is not about the bike pump.

It goes the way these go: a fair point, a defensive answer, a louder fair point, the word "always", a door. Twenty minutes later he is on the step and she is sitting on box seventeen, which is still not unpacked, which is also somehow his fault.

What they do next is the only part that matters. He comes back in, which is the rule. She says the true sentence: that was rough and I still like you. They do the washing up without talking about it. On Day 2 she writes "the thing I was actually upset about was feeling like this is your flat with me in it", and on Day 4 he reads it back to her before he says anything else, and gets it right. The bike pump is still a problem. It is now a small one.

Honest limits

This is a week of small moves for an ordinary fight between two people who want to stay. It is not couples therapy and it is not for a relationship where one person is frightened of the other. The Gottman Institute's four horsemen (criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling) are a better self-check than any web page; if contempt is a regular visitor, talk to a counsellor. On the numbers, Pew Research's report on marriage and cohabitation is where the "most couples live together first" claim comes from. Questions couples ask

Is it normal to fight after moving in together?

Yes, common enough that most of the advice pages on the subject treat it as a stage rather than a sign. Two people with two sets of habits now share one fridge, one bathroom and one definition of "clean". Pew Research found that most US adults who have lived with a partner did so before or instead of marriage, so this first-fight week is a near-universal rite. What matters is how the week after the fight goes, not whether it happened.

How long does the adjustment period take?

Nobody can give you a number that is honest. Most couples we have talked with say the sharp edges dull within a few months, and that the first month is the worst because every habit is new at once. The plan above is for the week after the first fight, not a cure for the quarter.

What if we moved in too soon?

One fight does not tell you that. A pattern of contempt, name-calling, or one person always walking out and never coming back, does, and that is worth a conversation with a counsellor rather than a web page. The Gottman Institute's piece on the four horsemen is a fair place to check your own behaviour, not just your partner's.

Does UnFight work if only one of us uses it?

Yes. Everything in it is private to the phone it is on. The pause, the words, the RESET method and the next-step choice are all for one person. Partner Mode, where you pass one phone back and forth, needs both of you to tap yes, and nothing is shown to the other person unless you choose.

What if an argument feels unsafe?

If you choose "I don't feel safe continuing" inside the app, it stops the ordinary flow and shows safety resources instead. If you are in danger now, contact local emergency services. This page is general information about ordinary disagreements, not advice for abuse.

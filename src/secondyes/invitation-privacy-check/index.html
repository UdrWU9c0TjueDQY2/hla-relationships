Which details belong in this invitation?

You are arranging a café date at a public venue with someone you already know. Select only the details you would put in the invitation, then reveal the reasoning. These are fictional details; no message is sent.

Include the plan.

A recipient should be able to understand where you propose meeting, when and for roughly how long. Use the public meeting location, not an unrelated private address. If there is a ticket cost or reservation requirement, make that clear before someone agrees. Verify those details directly rather than relying on a saved idea.

Offer an answer that does not require a yes. Another time or a simple decline should remain a real option. A link makes a plan easier to inspect; it does not create an obligation to accept it.

Leave out the explanation trail.

You can offer two free time slots without sharing your entire calendar. You can say “this time works for me” without attaching messages that explain why. Include only the context needed for this plan; discuss a genuinely necessary personal detail separately with the intended person.

Before forwarding a screenshot, check the visible names, notifications, other conversations and location details around the plan. Cropping is not proof that every sensitive detail has been removed. Read the actual image before sharing it.

The five-check sharing pause

  1. Content: Does the invitation contain only information needed for this plan?
  2. Recipient: Is this the intended person and the intended conversation, rather than a group or public post?
  3. Link access: Could someone else open the link if it were forwarded?
  4. Choice: Can the other person suggest a different time or decline without an explanation?
  5. Confirmation: Are the final place and time agreed before either person travels?

This checklist reduces unnecessary disclosure. It cannot guarantee privacy or control what another person copies, forwards or records.

Does “private link” mean only one person can open it?

Not necessarily. For SecondYes specifically, the App Store description says invitation details travel inside the shared link and anyone with that link can open it. Treat possession of the link as access to its invitation details. Do not assume the link is an authenticated, recipient-only space.

Can I revoke or password-protect a SecondYes link?

This resource does not claim those features. Check the current app before relying on an access-control feature. If a link has been shared more widely than intended, avoid assuming that removing a message removes copies other people already hold.

Is this a dating-safety assessment?

No. This is a content and sharing checklist for an invitation. It does not assess a person, guarantee safety or replace your own judgement about whether to meet.

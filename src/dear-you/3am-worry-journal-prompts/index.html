Questions people ask at 3am

Does writing down worries at night actually help you sleep?

For many people, yes: putting a to-do or a worry on paper takes it off the loop. NHS sleep guidance suggests writing worries or a to-do list before bed so you are less likely to lie awake going over them. It is not a cure for insomnia, and if 3am is every night for weeks, that is a GP conversation. This is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your clinician.

Should I get up or stay in bed?

The usual advice is not to lie there fighting it: if you have been awake a while, get up, keep the lights low, do something dull, and go back when sleepy. The checklist above is built for that twenty minutes.

What is the point of sealing a letter?

A worry with a date on it is a worry you are allowed to put down. Writing "open Thursday at 9" turns tonight's loop into Thursday's task. In Dear You the sealed letter stays locked until its day, even from you, then opens in the app; the paper version is an envelope on the kitchen table.

I worry about the same thing every night. What then?

Then the flowchart will keep sending you to the same prompt, which is information: it is either a thing to act on in daylight or a thing to talk through with someone. Sealing it for a week and reading it back is a decent way to see which. A composite scenario, not a customer story.

Hana is awake because of an email she has not sent, which has become, between 1 and 3, an email she cannot send, a job she is bad at, and a vague sense that everyone knows. The phone is on the pillow. It has been very helpful about none of this.

The flowchart asks whether there is something she can do tomorrow. There is: send the email. One thing. The prompt says write the next action in one sentence and the time. "Send the draft to Priya at 9:30, before the stand-up." Then: "Everything else about this can wait until 9:30." She seals it for 9:30. The phone goes in the hall. The water takes three minutes. She is back in bed by 3:40 and asleep by something, because she stopped checking.

At 9:30 the letter opens. The email takes four minutes. The job she is bad at turns out to be a Tuesday night.

How it works, in three steps

  1. Open a letter, or an envelope. Pick what you need tonight: calm, courage, a fresh start. Or open the sealed "when you can't sleep" envelope from the Open When shelf.

01 / MAKE IT SPECIFIC

“The blue cup” beats “a nice day.”

Choose one thing you could point to. A sound from the next room. The label on a jar. A half-finished task. Details give your later self something concrete to return to.

02 / LEAVE ROOM

You do not need a lesson.

A plain sentence is enough. You can leave a question unanswered or describe a day that felt ordinary. Nothing needs a positive ending or a promise to change.

03 / KEEP A COPY

Choose where it belongs.

Download a text file, print a page, or copy your words into a journal you already use. This browser page has no storage and cannot deliver a future reminder.

A one-minute writing exercise

Listen to a room

On paper, put yourself in the center and mark up to three sounds around you. If listening is difficult, use visible movements. Pick one close detail and one farther away. Write two sentences to your future self about what changed between them.

Fictional example. “My spoon tapped the cup twice before the bus doors hissed shut outside. I want to remember how quiet breakfast felt in the pause between them.”

A few practical questions

Where does my writing go?

The worksheet keeps your draft only in this page’s memory. Its code does not send or store your answers. Your browser or device may have its own autofill or recovery features. Downloaded files remain wherever you save them; normal hosting access logs may exist.

Will this letter arrive in my email later?

No. There is no email delivery or future scheduling here. The download is a plain text file that you can open immediately.

Is one sentence enough?

Yes. Answer any one of the four main prompts to enable downloading. The day’s name alone does not count as a completed prompt.
